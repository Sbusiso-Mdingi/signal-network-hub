Governance

Governance is not a disclaimer. It is part of the operating model.

Claims-integrity technology works in a sensitive environment. Sequrin is designed so analytical capability, access, investigation and collaboration each operate inside explicit boundaries rather than collapsing into one automated decision system.

The governing idea: analytical capability can increase without silently expanding decision authority.

Core principles

Six boundaries shape how Sequrin should be used.

01

Signals are investigative, not determinative

Rules, models, statistical methods and network analysis may surface activity worth examining. Their output is a signal, not a finding of fraud or misconduct.

02

Detection has no claims authority

A detection result cannot itself reject, delay, redirect or withhold a claim or benefit payment, and cannot impose a sanction.

03

Consequential judgement remains human

Evidence, context and accountable organisational processes remain responsible for conclusions and consequential decisions.

04

Access follows role and organisation

Sensitive claims-integrity information should only be available to appropriately authorised people within controlled organisational boundaries.

05

Important actions should be traceable

Investigation history, access decisions and significant administrative actions should support review, accountability and auditability.

06

Collaboration requires separate governance

Cross-organisational sharing is not an informal extension of an investigation. Any shared-network action requires its own governed process.

Decision boundary

Analytics can prioritise attention. They do not inherit decision authority.

Analytical layer

What Sequrin may help surface

Sequrin can assemble investigative context and help authorised teams decide what deserves closer examination.

  • Unusual behaviour and priority signals
  • Connected provider, member and facility relationships
  • Chronology and structured evidence context
  • Reasons an activity pattern deserves review

Accountable authority

What remains outside detection

Consequential conclusions and actions remain outside the authority of analytical detection.

  • Fraud or misconduct findings
  • Claim or payment decisions
  • Sanctions and contractual consequences
  • Shared-network publication

Detection creates a reason to investigate. It does not create permission to punish.

Access governance

Sensitive access should be deliberate, scoped and reviewable.

Sequrin separates identity from operational authority. Being able to sign in does not, by itself, mean a person should be able to see every investigation, administer every user or perform every privileged action.

01Organisation boundary

Operational data and workforce access remain scoped to the relevant organisation rather than becoming globally visible by default.

02Explicit authority

Access to sensitive functions should follow assigned responsibilities rather than broad platform membership alone.

03Administrative separation

Managing access is distinct from making investigative findings, reducing the chance that one workflow silently grants another form of authority.

04Traceable change

Important role, permission and administrative changes should leave evidence that can be reviewed later.

Investigation and publication

A finding inside one organisation is not automatically a network publication.

Sequrin deliberately separates investigation from cross-organisational publication. An investigator may gather evidence, record context and reach an internal conclusion through the organisation's accountable process. That does not grant direct authority to publish a shared network notice.

Govern separately

Any future shared-network publication follows an additional governed process rather than inheriting authority from the investigation.

Secure Integrity Network

Network collaboration needs a higher governance bar, not a lower one.

The longer-term Secure Integrity Network vision is not a shared blacklist. Cross-organisational integrity intelligence would need additional rules, controls and accountability beyond those required inside a single medical scheme.

Explore the network direction →

Any future network model should require

  • Clear participation and access rules
  • Defined evidence and publication standards
  • Purpose limitation and data-governance controls
  • Separation between investigation and network publication
  • Correction, challenge and review mechanisms
  • Auditability across participating organisations

Governance in one sentence

Sequrin can help people see more clearly, but authority remains with accountable people and governed organisations.

That boundary is not a limitation added after the product was built. It is part of what the product is intended to be.
